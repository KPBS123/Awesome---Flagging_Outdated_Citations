# Research Paper Collection

## 1. Foundational Papers

* **Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks**

  Patrick Lewis, Ethan Perez, Aleksandra Piktus, Fabio Petroni, Vladimir Karpukhin, Naman Goyal, Heinrich Küttler, Mike Lewis, Wen-tau Yih, Tim Rocktäschel, Sebastian Riedel, and Douwe Kiela, 2020, *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS)*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2005.11401)

  This paper introduces Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which is relevant to retrieving current scientific literature and generating evidence-based citation recommendations.

* **Content-Based Citation Recommendation**

  Chandra Bhagavatula, Sergey Feldman, Russell Power, and Waleed Ammar, 2018, *Proceedings of NAACL-HLT 2018*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N18-1022)

  This paper presents a content-based method for automatically recommending citations for academic papers, making it directly relevant to citation recommendation.

* **SciBERT: A Pretrained Language Model for Scientific Text**

  Iz Beltagy, Kyle Lo, and Arman Cohan, 2019, *Proceedings of EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1371)

  SciBERT is a scientific-domain language model that can be used to understand and classify scientific text and citation contexts.

* **Structural Scaffolds for Citation Intent Classification in Scientific Publications**

  Arman Cohan, Waleed Ammar, Madeleine van Zuylen, and Field Cady, 2019, *Proceedings of NAACL-HLT 2019*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N19-1361)

  This paper introduces citation-intent classification and the SciCite dataset, which can help determine why a particular citation is being used before evaluating whether it should be replaced.

## 2. Survey and Review Papers

* **Citation Recommendation: Approaches and Datasets**

  Michael Färber and Adam Jatowt, 2020, *International Journal on Digital Libraries*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00799-020-00288-2)

  This survey reviews citation recommendation approaches, datasets, and evaluation methods and provides important background for developing a citation recommendation system.

* **A Review of Citation Recommendation: From Textual Content to Enriched Context**

  Shutian Ma, Chengzhi Zhang, and Xiaozhong Liu, 2020, *Scientometrics*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-019-03336-0)

  This review examines how citation recommendation systems use textual content, citation networks, and other contextual information to improve recommendations.

* **Scholarly Recommendation Systems: A Literature Survey**

  Zitong Zhang, Braja Gopal Patra, Ashraf Yaseen, Jie Zhu, Rachit Sabharwal, Kirk Roberts, Tru Cao, and Hulin Wu, 2023, *Knowledge and Information Systems*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-023-01901-x)

  This survey analyzes scholarly recommendation systems and provides background on methods used for recommending academic literature and citations.

## 3. Recent Research Papers

* **ILCiteR: Evidence-grounded Interpretable Local Citation Recommendation**

  Sayar Ghosh Roy and Jiawei Han, 2024, *Proceedings of LREC-COLING 2024*

  [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.757/)

  This paper proposes an interpretable citation recommendation system that provides evidence supporting why a particular paper should be cited.

* **Paper Recommendation Using Citation Contexts in Scholarly Documents**

  Tomoki Ikoma and Shigeki Matsubara, 2023, *Proceedings of the 37th Pacific Asia Conference on Language, Information and Computation (PACLIC)*

  [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.paclic-1.71/)

  This research uses citation contexts to recommend scholarly papers, which is relevant to finding papers that better match the meaning and purpose of an existing citation.

* **Exploiting Citation Knowledge in Personalised Recommendation of Recent Scientific Publications**

  Anita Khadka, Iván Cantador, and Miriam Fernandez, 2020, *Proceedings of the Twelfth Language Resources and Evaluation Conference (LREC 2020)*

  [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.272/)

  This paper uses citation graphs, citation positions, citation contexts, and citation types to recommend recent scientific publications, making it particularly relevant to finding newer citation alternatives.

* **Recommending Missed Citations Identified by Reviewers: A New Task, Dataset and Baselines**

  Kehan Long, Shasha Li, Pancheng Wang, Chenlong Bao, Jintao Tang, and Ting Wang, 2024, *Proceedings of LREC-COLING 2024*

  [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1196/)

  This paper introduces a task and dataset for identifying citations that are missing from research papers, making it relevant to improving citation completeness and quality.

## 4. Methods / Algorithms

* **Citation Resolution: A Method for Evaluating Context-Based Citation Recommendation Systems**

  Daniel Duma and Ewan Klein, 2014, *Proceedings of ACL 2014*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/P14-2059)

  This paper presents an evaluation method for context-based citation recommendation systems and is useful for evaluating the performance of a citation recommendation model.

* **Applying Core Scientific Concepts to Context-Based Citation Recommendation**

  Daniel Duma, Maria Liakata, Amanda Clare, James Ravenscroft, and Ewan Klein, 2016, *Proceedings of LREC 2016*

  [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/L16-1274/)

  This paper uses scientific concepts and citation context to improve citation recommendation, providing methods for understanding the relationship between text and recommended papers.

## 5. Applications / Scientific Evidence

* **Fact or Fiction: Verifying Scientific Claims**

  David Wadden, Shanchuan Lin, Kyle Lo, Lucy Lu Wang, Madeleine van Zuylen, Arman Cohan, and Hannaneh Hajishirzi, 2020, *Proceedings of EMNLP 2020*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.609)

  This paper introduces SciFact for verifying scientific claims using evidence from research literature, which is relevant to determining whether cited research actually supports a claim.

* **SciFact-Open: Towards Open-Domain Scientific Claim Verification**

  David Wadden, Kyle Lo, Bailey Kuehl, Arman Cohan, Iz Beltagy, Lucy Lu Wang, and Hannaneh Hajishirzi, 2022, *Findings of EMNLP 2022*

  [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.347/)

  This work extends scientific claim verification to a large corpus of 500,000 research abstracts, making it relevant to large-scale retrieval and verification of scientific evidence.

## 6. Evaluation Methods / Benchmarks

* **Modeling the Obsolescence of Research Literature in Disciplinary Journals Through the Age of Their Cited References**

  Pablo Dorta-González and Emilio Gómez-Déniz, 2022, *Scientometrics*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-022-04359-w)

  This paper directly studies literature obsolescence and citation aging across scientific disciplines, providing an important foundation for identifying potentially outdated citations.

* **S2ORC: The Semantic Scholar Open Research Corpus**

  Kyle Lo, Lucy Lu Wang, Mark Neumann, Rodney Kinney, and Daniel Weld, 2020, *Proceedings of ACL 2020*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.447)

  S2ORC provides large-scale scientific papers, metadata, references, and citation information that can be used to train and evaluate citation-analysis systems.

* **unarXive: A Large Scholarly Data Set with Publications' Full-Text, Annotated In-Text Citations, and Links to Metadata**

  Tarek Saier and Michael Färber, 2020, *Scientometrics*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-020-03382-z)

  unarXive provides more than one million scientific documents with annotated citation contexts and linked metadata, making it useful for training and evaluating citation-analysis and recommendation systems.

* **Construction of the Literature Graph in Semantic Scholar**

  Waleed Ammar, Dirk Groeneveld, Chandra Bhagavatula, Iz Beltagy, Miles Crawford, Doug Downey, Jason Dunkelberger, Ahmed Elgohary, Sergey Feldman, Vu Ha, Rodney Kinney, Sebastian Kohlmeier, Kyle Lo, Tyler Murray, Hsu-Han Ooi, Matthew Peters, Joanna Power, Sam Skjonsberg, Lucy Lu Wang, Chris Wilhelm, Zheng Yuan, Madeleine van Zuylen, and Oren Etzioni, 2018, *Proceedings of NAACL-HLT 2018*

  [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/N18-3011)

  This paper describes a large scholarly literature graph containing papers, authors, citations, and other relationships, providing useful infrastructure for citation-network analysis and recommendation.
